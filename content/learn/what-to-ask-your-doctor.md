Appointments are short. Being specific is the difference between leaving with answers and leaving with "it's probably stress."

This is not medical advice, and your doctor may have good reasons to do something different. It is a starting point so that you walk in prepared instead of hoping the right question occurs to you in the ten minutes you have.

## Ask for these by name

**Ferritin.** Your iron stores. This is the most important one for hair, and the one most often left off a standard panel. Ask for the number, not whether it is normal.

**Full blood count.** Standard, but it establishes whether there is anaemia on top of low stores.

**Thyroid function — TSH, plus free T4.** Thyroid problems are a well-recognised cause of hair loss and are very treatable once identified. TSH alone can miss things.

**Vitamin D.** Widely low, particularly in northern Europe and particularly in winter.

**Androgens — total testosterone, free testosterone, DHEA-S and SHBG.** Relevant if you also have acne, unusually irregular cycles, or hair growth where you would rather not have it. These are the results that point toward PCOS.

**Zinc, and vitamin B12.** Worth including if the pattern is unexplained shedding.

## Ask for the numbers themselves

Request a copy of your results. Not the interpretation — the actual figures with the reference ranges beside them.

There is a real difference between a ferritin of 15 and a ferritin of 80. Both may be reported as "normal". Only one of them is a plausible contributor to hair loss. If all you are given is the word normal, you cannot tell which situation you are in.

Keep them. Comparing this year's numbers to last year's is often more informative than either on its own.

## How to describe what is happening

Doctors respond to specifics. Vague descriptions get vague answers.

- **When it started**, and what else was happening then — an illness, a stressful period, a new medication, a birth, a big weight change, coming off or starting hormonal contraception.
- **The pattern.** All over, or at the parting and temples? Shedding from the root, or breaking along the length? These point in genuinely different directions.
- **How much.** "Clumps in the shower every morning for four months" lands differently to "a lot."
- **What else changed.** Skin, cycles, digestion, energy, sleep, mood. Mention all of it, even if it seems unrelated. Especially if it seems unrelated — that pattern is often the most useful thing you will say.
- **Bring your photos.** If you have been taking monthly pictures, show them.

## If you are dismissed

It happens, and it is not a reflection of whether something is wrong.

You are allowed to ask for the specific tests to be recorded as requested and, if declined, to have the reason noted. You are allowed to ask for a referral to a dermatologist or gynaecologist. You are allowed to see a different doctor.

Our founder saw several over a number of years before anyone looked properly. Persistence is an unfair thing to have to summon when you are already exhausted, and it is often what it takes.
