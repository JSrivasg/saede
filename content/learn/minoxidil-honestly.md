Minoxidil is the treatment most women are offered first, often as the only suggestion, and frequently without much explanation attached.

We are writing about it for two reasons. Our founder was offered it, with no context, by a doctor who had not looked for a cause. And a brand that only ever explains the option it happens to sell is not an education hub, it is a brochure.

**To be completely clear about where we stand: minoxidil is a medicine. Saede is a food supplement. We are not an alternative to it, we do not claim to do what it does, and nothing on this page should be read as a reason to stop or avoid a treatment your doctor has recommended.**

## What it is

Minoxidil started life as a blood pressure medication. Patients taking it grew more hair, which turned out to be more commercially interesting than its original purpose, and it was developed into a topical treatment applied to the scalp.

It is available as a liquid or foam in various strengths, and in some countries as a low-dose oral tablet prescribed off-label by dermatologists. It is one of very few treatments for pattern hair loss with a substantial body of clinical evidence behind it, and for many people it works.

## What it does, and does not do

Minoxidil acts on the hair follicle and the growth cycle — extending the growing phase and encouraging follicles into it.

What it does not do is address a cause. It does not change your iron levels, your thyroid, your androgens or your stress. If something underneath is driving the loss, minoxidil works despite that thing rather than resolving it. This is precisely why the "have you tried minoxidil?" conversation is so frustrating when it arrives *instead of* an investigation rather than alongside one.

## The trade-offs people are not always told

**You have to keep using it.** Its effect depends on continued use. Stop, and hair gained is generally lost over the following months. This is the single most important thing to understand before starting, because it is a long-term commitment and it is often mentioned only in passing.

**Shedding at the start.** Many people shed more in the first weeks. It is expected and usually temporary — it reflects follicles being pushed into a new cycle — but if nobody warns you, it is alarming and a common reason people stop early.

**Scalp irritation.** Itching, dryness and flaking are fairly common with the topical form, sometimes from the propylene glycol in the liquid rather than the drug itself. The foam suits some people better.

**Unwanted hair elsewhere.** Occasionally on the face, particularly with the oral form.

**Daily application, forever.** Practically, this is what people find hardest.

**The oral form needs supervision.** It is a blood pressure medication. Off-label low-dose oral minoxidil is prescribed by dermatologists with monitoring, and is not something to source yourself.

## If you are considering it

Talk to a doctor or dermatologist — ideally after you have had the blood tests, so that any underlying cause has been looked for rather than skipped. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are specific contraindications; ask.

Some people use minoxidil and take supplements. That is a question for your doctor, not for us, and we would rather say so than pretend to an expertise we do not have.
